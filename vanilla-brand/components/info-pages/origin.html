Origin of Vanilla

Vanilla was first cultivated by the Totonac people on the Gulf Coast of Mexico, who prized the orchid's pods as a sacred offering and a luxurious flavoring for cacao drinks long before Europeans arrived.

After the Spanish conquest, vanilla pods made their way to Europe, and vanilla orchids were later carried to islands like Réunion and Madagascar — but the plant stubbornly refused to bear fruit, since its one natural pollinator, the Melipona bee, only lived in Mexico.

In 1841, on the French island of Réunion, a 12-year-old enslaved boy named Edmond Albius discovered that the flowers could be hand-pollinated with a simple quick touch of a thin stick and thumb. His technique is still used around the world today and is the reason vanilla could finally be grown far from home.
